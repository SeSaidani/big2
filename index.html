          Turn alerts

          If it is your turn and you have not played after 20 minutes, this phone gets an alert, and one more after 24 hours. It never shows your cards. Nobody else sees anything.

          Devices with alerts on

            Not getting alerts?
            • Press Send a test alert. If it arrives, everything works and the game just has not reached your turn yet. Alerts only come after 20 minutes of not playing.
            • Blocked? If you once tapped Block, the phone remembers. Android: open Settings, Apps, your browser, Notifications, and allow them. iPhone: Settings, Notifications, Big Two, Allow Notifications.
            • Silent? Do Not Disturb, a Focus mode or battery saver can hold alerts back. Add Big Two to the allowed apps, or turn the mode off.
            • Start again. Press Turn off for this device, then Turn on alerts. On an iPhone you can also delete the Home Screen icon, add the game to the Home Screen again, sign in and turn alerts on.
            • New phone or browser? Alerts belong to one phone and browser. Sign in there and turn them on there too.

            Get turn alerts on your iPhone

            To get an alert when it is your turn, the game has to live on your Home Screen first. It takes about a minute:

            1. Open this page in Safari (not inside another app).
            2. Tap the Share button (the square with an arrow, at the bottom of the screen).
            3. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen, then Add.
            4. Open Big Two from the new icon on your Home Screen.
            5. Sign in again there (the icon keeps its own sign-in), then tap Turn on alerts when it asks.

            Needs iOS 16.4 or newer. You can find these steps again under your name, then Turn alerts.

            How to play

            The aim

            Be the first to get rid of all your cards. Everyone else pays you for the cards they are left holding.

            The cards

            56 cards: a standard deck plus 4 wild jokers. Ranks run 3 (lowest) up to K, A and 2 (highest). Suits run ♦ < ♣ < ♥ < ♠. With 4 players everyone gets 14 cards. With 3 players (solo only) everyone gets 18, and the 2 spare cards go to whoever holds the 3♦ and whoever holds the 2♠ (so they start with 19, or one player with 20).

            Playing

            • The holder of the 3♦ opens with a play that includes it.
            • After that, play the same kind of hand as the last play, but higher: a single, a pair, a triple or five cards.
            • Five-card hands rank: straight, flush, full house, four of a kind (plus any card), straight flush, five of a kind. A straight counts by its highest card. In A-2-3-4-5 and 2-3-4-5-6 the ace and the 2 are low, and these are the two weakest straights.
            • You may pass at any time, even when you have the lead. Once you pass on a trick you are out of it until it starts again. If nobody left can beat a play, the others are passed for you automatically.
            • If the next player has one card left, a single you play must be your highest.

            Jokers

            A joker stands for any card that makes your hand strongest. A real card always beats a joker standing for the same thing, so nothing beats the real 2♠. A lone joker counts as a 2♠ but loses to the real one.

            Scoring

            When someone goes out, each of the others pays them their number of cards left times a rate that grows with the count. Going out with a straight flush or five of a kind doubles every payment. Points always add up to zero.

            Cards left (4 players) Rate per card Cards left (3 players)
            1 to 7 1 1 to 9
            8 to 10 2 10 to 13
            11 to 13 3 14 to 17
            14 4 18 to 20

            Luck & skill

            In the leaderboard, the Luck & skill view shows how each player did against what their dealt cards were worth, so a run of good or bad hands is not mistaken for good or bad play. It compares you with Medium-level play from the same cards, so it is a yardstick and not a measure of absolute skill. It needs 10 games before it shows your figures, and counts only games saved with their deals.

            Solo

            Play against the computer, any time. Press New game to choose 3 or 4 players and Easy, Medium or Hard. The settings are fixed for the whole game. Solo games are for practice: they are not scored on the leaderboard.

            Online

            • One game runs at a time. An admin creates it, while a season is running, and only an admin can cancel it.
            • A season game needs 4 people. It starts when every seat is taken by a person: there are no computer players online. For those, play Solo.
            • Leaving to the lobby keeps your seat. Nobody is timed out: if a player is away, the game waits for them.
            • Chat appears once two or more people are at the table.
            • Turn on alerts (under your name, then Turn alerts) and your phone buzzes if it has been your turn for 20 minutes, and once more after 24 hours. On an iPhone, add the game to the Home Screen first.
            • The leaderboard adds up points over a season. Only online season games count towards it.
